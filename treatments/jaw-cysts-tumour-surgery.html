Jaw Cysts & Tumour Surgery

Accurate diagnosis and surgical removal of jaw cysts and tumours, often discovered incidentally on a routine X-ray.

Call Now WhatsApp Us
About the Procedure

What it involves

Jaw cysts and tumours are frequently found by chance during a dental X-ray, long before they cause symptoms. Diagnosis comes first — through imaging and, where indicated, biopsy — followed by a surgical plan sized to the growth itself, since jaw surgery ranges from a small, straightforward enucleation to a more extensive procedure depending on size and location.

Benefits

Why patients choose this treatment

  • Accurate diagnosis before any surgical decision is made
  • Surgical approach matched to the size and location of the growth
  • Protects surrounding nerves, teeth and bone structure
  • Follow-up care to monitor healing and lab results
Procedure

What to expect, step by step

  1. DiagnosisImaging and, where needed, a biopsy confirm the nature of the growth.
  2. Surgical PlanningThe removal approach is planned around size, location and proximity to nerves.
  3. Surgical RemovalThe cyst or tumour is carefully removed under appropriate anaesthesia.
  4. Follow-UpHealing is monitored and biopsy results are reviewed with you.
Who Needs It

Signs this treatment may help you

  • A cyst identified incidentally on a dental X-ray
  • A growth or swelling noticed in the jaw or gum
  • Persistent unexplained swelling in the oral cavity
  • A referral for a suspicious jaw lesion
Recovery

Healing & aftercare

Recovery depends on the size and location of the growth removed. Mild swelling is normal in the days after surgery, with follow-up visits scheduled to monitor healing and review any lab findings.
